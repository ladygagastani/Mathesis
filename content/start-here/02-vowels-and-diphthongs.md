# Vowels and diphthongs

Long and short vowels, the eight pairs of vowels said as one sound, and two small signs.

## Long and short

A long vowel is held about twice as long as a short one. English does not use length to tell
words apart, but Greek does: λύομεν *lyomen* is "we set free" and λύωμεν *lyōmen* is "let us
set free". Only the length of one vowel separates them.

| Always short | Always long | Either |
|---|---|---|
| ε, ο | η, ω | α, ι, υ |

For α, ι and υ the spelling does not show which. Two clues help:

- a vowel with the wavy accent ( ῀ , the **circumflex**, step 3) is long: ᾶ;
- a vowel with a small iota under it is long: ᾳ.

Learners' books mark the rest: a line on top for long (ᾱ, a **macron**), a small cup for
short (ᾰ, a **breve**).

## Diphthongs

A **diphthong** is two vowels said together as one syllable, like *oi* in *coin*. In Greek
the second vowel is always ι or υ.

| Diphthong | Written as | Say it like | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| αι | ai | "igh", as in *s<u>igh</u>*, or "ai", as in *<u>ai</u>sle* | καί *kai* "and" |
| αυ | au | "ow", as in *c<u>ow</u>* | αὐτός *autos* "self" |
| ει | ei | "ey", as in *th<u>ey</u>* | εἰμί *eimi* "I am" |
| ευ | eu | "ey", as in *th<u>ey</u>*, run into a French "u". For a feel of it, say *h<u>e</u>y, <u>you</u>* as one syllable | εὖ *eu* "well" |
| ηυ | ēu | the same as ευ, with a longer "e": a longer *h<u>e</u>y <u>you</u>* | ηὔδα *ēuda* "he spoke" |
| οι | oi | "oi", as in *c<u>oi</u>n* | οἶκος *oikos* "house" |
| ου | ou | "oo", as in *p<u>oo</u>l* | οὐ *ou* "not" |
| υι | ui | the "ew" sound (the French "u") run into an "i", as in *pol<u>i</u>ce*. For a feel of it, say *st<u>ewie</u>* or *ch<u>ewy</u>* | υἱός *huios* "son" |

In these pairs υ is written *u*, not *y*.

**How to spot a diphthong.** Breathings and accents (step 3) sit on its *second* vowel:
οἶκος, αὐτός, εἰμί. Two vowels that do not end in ι or υ are two syllables: θεός is
*the-os*.

## Two small signs

- **An iota under a vowel** (ᾳ, ῃ, ῳ) is silent: read ᾳ, ῃ, ῳ as long *ā*, *ē*, *ō*. It is
  written *āi*, *ēi*, *ōi*: τῷ λόγῳ *tōi logōi* "to the word". In capitals the iota stands
  beside the letter instead: ᾠδή *ōidē* "song" is written ΩΙΔΗ.
- **Two dots over ι or υ** (ϊ, ϋ) mean "not a diphthong": say that vowel on its own, as in
  English *naïve*. The first line of the *Iliad* has one: Πηληϊάδεω *Pē-lē-i-a-deō* "Peleus'
  son".

## Practice

**1. Read aloud,** then check.

αὐτός · οὐρανός · εὖ · ζῷον · ηὔδα · καί

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

- αὐτός *autos*: αυ is "ow", as in *cow*.
- οὐρανός *ouranos* "sky": ου is "oo", as in *pool*.
- εὖ *eu* "well": one syllable, like a quick *hey you*.
- ζῷον *zōion* "animal": say *zdō-on*; the iota under ω is silent.
- ηὔδα *ēuda* "he spoke" ([*Odyssey* 1.213](read:tlg0012.tlg002:1.213)): like εὖ, with a longer "e".
- καί *kai* "and": "igh", as in *sigh*.
</details>

**2. Diphthong or not?** For each word, say whether the vowels in question form a diphthong.

οἶκος · προΐαψεν · υἱός · Ἄϊδι · θεός · ᾠδή

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

- οἶκος: yes, οι (the accent sits on its second letter).
- προΐαψεν: no; the two dots separate ο and ι: *pro-i-ap-sen*.
- υἱός: yes, υι.
- Ἄϊδι: no (two dots): *A-i-di*.
- θεός: no. A diphthong must end in ι or υ, so εο cannot be one: *the-os*.
- ᾠδή: no. ῳ is a long *ō* with a silent iota under it.
</details>

**3. Say these two words aloud:** λύομεν and λύωμεν. What separates them?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Only vowel length: short ο in *lyomen* "we set free", long ω in *lyōmen* "let us set
free". A Modern Greek speaker says both *líomen*, because vowel length has since
disappeared (step 3).
</details>

## For review (not for publication)
- **New diphthong cues (25 Sep 2026),** from the editor's list: αι "igh" (sigh) / "ai" (aisle); αυ "ow" (cow); ει "ey" (they); ευ "ey" + French *u* (*hey, you*); ηυ the same with a longer *e*; οι "oi" (coin); ου "oo" (pool); υι "ew" + "i" (police; *Stewie*, *chewy*). Previous cues: αι "ai in aisle"; ει "ey in they, held steady (not the same as η)"; υι "French *ui* in *lui*"; αυ "ow in how"; ευ "e of pet + oo, in one syllable"; ου "oo in food".
- **ει and η.** The old table warned that ει is "not the same as η". With the new cues ει is *they* and η is *bear*, so they are still distinct; check you are happy with how close they sound to a beginner.
- **ει and ου.** The page gives their sounds as in Plato's time: ει a long closed *e*, ου already a long *oo*. Some teachers use [oː] for ου in the 5th century. Allen dates the change to *oo* to around the 4th century BCE.
- **Iota subscript.** "Medieval scribes" was deliberately vague on the old page, and is now left out. The subscript is usually dated to about the 12th century. The ι had stopped being pronounced by the later Hellenistic period.
- **Pindar's heading.** One heading in the app's Pindar text prints ΣΥΡΑΚΟΥΣΙῼ, with the small iota beside the capital (a third convention). I left it out to keep the explanation simple.
- **Dropped for length (24 Sep 2026):** the ἔθος/ἦθος and ἔπᾰθον examples and the Attic long-α rule (χώρᾱ).
- **Πηληϊάδεω.** I added the gloss "Peleus' son" (strictly genitive, "of Peleus' son"). Ἄϊδι and προΐαψεν (both in *Iliad* 1.3) now appear only in the exercise.
