# The alphabet and its sounds

The 24 letters, how to say each one, and the few that are not what they seem.

Many Greek letters look familiar, because our alphabet came from Greek by way of the Romans.
Watch for four false friends:

- ρ is *r*, not *p*
- η is a long *e*, not *n*
- ν is *n*, not *v*
- χ is *kh*, not *x*

## The breathy letters

θ, φ and χ were once *t*, *p* and *k* with a strong puff of air after them. Greek wrote the
puffed sounds with letters of their own:

| Plain | Breathy |
|---|---|
| τ *t* | θ *th* |
| π *p* | φ *ph* |
| κ *k* | χ *kh* |

Hold your hand in front of your mouth and say *potato*: you feel a small puff after the *p*.
For φ, make that puff stronger. For π, leave it out, as in the *p* of *spin*. The same goes
for θ and τ, and for χ and κ. Many readers say χ as the *ch* of *loch*, as in the table: that
also keeps it apart from κ, which is what matters.

The Romans wrote the breathy letters *ph*, *th* and *ch*, which is why English has
*philosophy*, *theatre* and *chorus* (with a *k* sound).

## Consonants: five things to know

- **γ before γ, κ, ξ or χ is "ng",** and is written *n*: ἄγγελος *angelos* "messenger"
  (English *angel*).
- **ζ, ξ and ψ are two sounds each:** "zd", "ks", "ps".
- **σ before β, γ, δ or μ sounds like "z":** κόσμος "order, world" is said *koz-mos*.
- **Sigma has two shapes:** σ inside a word, ς at the end: σοφός *sophos* "wise". Some
  editions use a third shape, ϲ, everywhere.
- **Double letters are held longer,** as in English *book-case*: θάλαττα *thalatta* "sea".

## Practice

> Ignore the small marks above the letters for now: step 3 explains them.

**1. Read aloud,** then check the transliteration and the notes.

θέατρον · φιλοσοφία · ἄγγελος · ψυχή · ξένος · χάος · κόσμος

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

- θέατρον *theatron*: θ is a breathy "t".
- φιλοσοφία *philosophia*: both φ are a breathy "p".
- ἄγγελος *angelos*: γγ is "ng-g".
- ψυχή *psykhē*: υ is the "ew" of *few*; χ is the *ch* of *loch*.
- ξένος *xenos*: "ks".
- χάος *khaos*: two syllables, *kha-os*.
- κόσμος *kosmos*: said *koz-mos*.
</details>

**2. Transliterate,** then find the English word each one gave us.

βιβλίον · ἄγκυρα · σφίγξ · χορός · κύκλος · ἠχώ · θρόνος

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

- *biblion* (book): *Bible*, *bibliography*
- *ankyra*: *anchor*
- *sphinx*: *sphinx*
- *khoros*: *chorus*, *choir*
- *kyklos* (circle): *cycle*
- *ēkhō*: *echo*
- *thronos* (seat): *throne*
</details>

- **Things that changed with the new cues.** (1) χ is now given as the *ch* of *loch* (a fricative), which is the Erasmian and Modern sound; in Plato's time it was a *k* with a puff. The breathy-letters section gives *spin* as the unpuffed *p*; decide whether the table itself should use *spin*, *stop*, *skin* instead. (3) "ew" in *few* is a *y* plus *oo*, not quite the French *u*; the table offers both.
