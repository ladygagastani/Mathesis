# The alphabet and its sounds

The Greek alphabet has 24 letters. Many look familiar, because our alphabet came from it
by way of the Romans. Watch for two false friends: ρ is *r* (not *p*), and η is a vowel
(not *n*). An evening's practice is enough to learn them.

**Which pronunciation?** These pages use the **reconstructed Classical Attic** sounds: our
best estimate of how Athenians spoke around 400 BCE. They keep apart the sounds the spelling
keeps apart, and they let you hear the rhythm of verse. The two other pronunciations you
will meet are summed up at the end of this page.

## The letters

"Long" vowels are held about twice as long as short ones (see *Vowel length* below).

| Letter | Name | Written as | Say it like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Α α | alpha | a | *a* in "father" (short or long) |
| Β β | beta | b | *b* in "bed" |
| Γ γ | gamma | g | *g* in "go"; *ng* before γ, κ, ξ, χ |
| Δ δ | delta | d | *d* in "dog" |
| Ε ε | epsilon | e | *e* in "pet"; always short |
| Ζ ζ | zeta | z | *zd* in "wisdom" |
| Η η | eta | ē | *e* in "bed", held long |
| Θ θ | theta | th | *t* in "top", with a puff of breath |
| Ι ι | iota | i | *ee* in "see" (short or long) |
| Κ κ | kappa | k | *k* in "skin", no puff |
| Λ λ | lambda | l | *l* |
| Μ μ | mu | m | *m* |
| Ν ν | nu | n | *n* |
| Ξ ξ | xi | x | *ks* in "box" |
| Ο ο | omicron | o | *o* in "hot"; always short |
| Π π | pi | p | *p* in "spin", no puff |
| Ρ ρ | rho | r | a rolled *r*, as in Spanish |
| Σ σ ς | sigma | s | *s* in "sun" |
| Τ τ | tau | t | *t* in "stop", no puff |
| Υ υ | upsilon | y | French *u* in *tu*: say *ee* with rounded lips |
| Φ φ | phi | ph | *p* in "pot", with a puff of breath |
| Χ χ | chi | kh | *k* in "kit", with a puff of breath |
| Ψ ψ | psi | ps | *ps* in "lapse" |
| Ω ω | omega | ō | *aw* in "saw", held long |

**The puff of breath.** Hold your hand in front of your mouth and say "pot", then "spot".
The *p* of "pot" comes with a puff of air; the *p* of "spot" does not. English never uses
this difference to tell words apart, but Greek does, with separate letters:

| No puff | With a puff |
|---|---|
| π (*p* in "spot") | φ (*p* in "pot") |
| τ (*t* in "stop") | θ (*t* in "top") |
| κ (*k* in "skit") | χ (*k* in "kit") |

The puffed sounds are called **aspirated** ("breathed on"). The Romans wrote them *ph*,
*th*, *ch*, which is why English has *philosophy*, *theatre* and *chorus* (with a *k*
sound). If the puff is hard at first, the *f* of "fun", the *th* of "thin" and the *ch* of
Scottish "loch" are an accepted stand-in: they still keep φ, θ, χ apart from π, τ, κ.

## Vowel length

A long vowel is held about twice as long as a short one. English does not use length to
tell words apart; Greek does. λύομεν *lyomen* is "we set free", λύωμεν *lyōmen* is "let us
set free", and only the length of one vowel separates them.

- **Always short:** ε, ο.
- **Always long:** η, ω.
- **Either:** α, ι, υ. The spelling does not show which. Learners' books mark long ones
  with a line on top (ᾱ ῑ ῡ, a **macron**) and short ones with a small cup (ᾰ ῐ ῠ, a
  **breve**).
- **Clues:** a vowel with a circumflex accent (ᾶ, page 3) is long, and so is a vowel with
  a small iota under it (ᾳ).

## Diphthongs

A **diphthong** is two vowels said together as one syllable, like *oi* in "coin". In Greek
the second vowel is always ι or υ.

| Diphthong | Written as | Say it like | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| αι | ai | *ai* in "aisle" | καί *kai* "and" |
| ει | ei | *ey* in "they", held steady (not the same as η) | εἰμί *eimi* "I am" |
| οι | oi | *oi* in "coin" | οἶκος *oikos* "house" |
| υι | ui | French *ui* in *lui* | υἱός *huios* "son" |
| αυ | au | *ow* in "how" | αὐτός *autos* "self" |
| ευ | eu | *e* of "pet" + *oo*, in one syllable | εὖ *eu* "well" |
| ηυ | ēu | the same, with a long *ē* | ηὔδα *ēuda* "he spoke" ([*Odyssey* 1.213](read:tlg0012.tlg002:1.213)) |
| ου | ou | *oo* in "food" | οὐ *ou* "not" |

**ᾳ, ῃ, ῳ.** The small iota written under a vowel (the **iota subscript**) is silent. Read
ᾳ, ῃ, ῳ as long ā, ē, ō: τῷ λόγῳ *tōi logōi* "to the word". In capitals the iota stands
beside the letter instead: ᾠδή *ōidē* "song" is written ΩΙΔΗ.

**How to spot a diphthong.** Breathings and accents (page 3) sit on the *second* vowel of a
diphthong: οἶκος, αὐτός, εἰμί.

**Two dots mean "not a diphthong".** Two dots over ι or υ (ϊ, ϋ), called a **diaeresis**,
tell you to say that vowel separately, as in English *naïve*. The *Iliad* has three in its
first three lines: Πηληϊάδεω *Pē-lē-i-a-deō*, Ἄϊδι *A-i-di*, προΐαψεν *pro-i-ap-sen*.

## Consonant tips

- **γ before γ, κ, ξ, χ is *ng*:** ἄγγελος *angelos* "messenger" (English *angel*),
  σφίγξ *sphinx*.
- **ζ, ξ, ψ are double sounds:** *zd*, *ks*, *ps*. In verse each counts as two consonants.
- **σ before β, γ, δ, μ sounds like *z*:** κόσμος "order, world" is said *koz-mos*.
- **Sigma has two shapes:** σ inside a word, ς at the end: σοφός *sophos* "wise". Some
  editions use a third, moon-shaped ϲ everywhere.
- **Double letters are held longer,** as in English "book-case": Ἀχιλλεύς *Akhilleus*,
  θάλαττα *thalatta* "sea".

## Other pronunciations you will hear

No one has heard Plato speak, so every pronunciation is a choice. The reconstructed one is
pieced together from ancient grammarians, spelling mistakes in inscriptions, and puns. One
comic poet has a fool "saying *bē bē* like a sheep", which shows η was not yet *ee*. The
standard account is W. Sidney Allen's *Vox Graeca* (3rd edition, 1987).

- **Erasmian**, the traditional school pronunciation (named after Erasmus, 1528). Close to
  the one above, but θ φ χ are said *th*, *f*, *ch* (loch), η is *ay* in "hay" and ω is
  *o* in "tone". It varies from country to country.
- **Modern Greek.** Greeks read ancient texts with today's sounds: β is *v*, δ is *th* in
  "this", θ is *th* in "thin", φ is *f*, χ is *ch* in "loch"; η, ι, υ, ει, οι all sound
  *ee*; αι is *e*; and vowel length is gone. Page 3 explains how the sounds changed.

## Practice

**1. Read aloud,** then check the transliteration and the notes below.

θέατρον · φιλοσοφία · ἄγγελος · ζῷον · ψυχή · ξένος · χάος · κόσμος · αὐτός · οὐρανός · ηὔδα

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

- θέατρον *theatron*: θ is the *t* of "top", with a puff.
- φιλοσοφία *philosophia*: both φ are the *p* of "pot", with a puff.
- ἄγγελος *angelos*: γγ is *ng-g*.
- ζῷον *zōion*: say *zdō-on*; the small iota is silent.
- ψυχή *psykhē*: υ like French *u*.
- ξένος *xenos*: *ks*.
- χάος *khaos*: two syllables, *kha-os*. αο is not a diphthong: a diphthong always ends in ι or υ.
- κόσμος *kosmos*: said *koz-mos*.
- αὐτός *autos*: *ow* as in "how".
- οὐρανός *ouranos* "sky": ου is *oo*.
- ηὔδα *ēuda*: long *ē* gliding into *oo*.
</details>

**2. Diphthong or not?** For each word, say whether the vowels in question form a diphthong.

οἶκος · προΐαψεν · αὐτός · υἱός · Ἄϊδι · θεός · ᾠδή · Πηληϊάδεω

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

- οἶκος: yes, οι (the accent sits on its second letter).
- προΐαψεν: no; the two dots separate ο and ι.
- αὐτός: yes, αυ.
- υἱός: yes, υι.
- Ἄϊδι: no (two dots).
- θεός: no. A diphthong must end in ι or υ, so εο cannot be one. Say *the-os*.
- ᾠδή: ῳ is a long ō with a silent iota.
- Πηληϊάδεω: no (two dots).
</details>

**3. Transliterate,** then find the English word each one gave us.

βιβλίον · ἄγκυρα · σφίγξ · χορός · κύκλος · ἠχώ · θρόνος

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

- *biblion* (book): *Bible*, *bibliography*
- *ankyra*: *anchor*
- *sphinx*: *sphinx*
- *khoros*: *chorus*, *choir*
- *kyklos* (circle): *cycle*
- *ēkhō*: *echo*
- *thronos* (seat): *throne*
</details>

**4. Say these two words aloud:** λύομεν and λύωμεν. What separates them? Would a Modern
Greek speaker hear any difference?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Only vowel length: short ο in *lyomen* "we set free", long ω in *lyōmen* "let us set
free". In Modern Greek both are said *líomen*, because vowel length has disappeared (page 3).
</details>

## For review (not for publication)
- **The sheep.** The line comes from the comic poet Cratinus: a fool goes about "saying βῆ βῆ like a sheep" (fr. 43 Kassel–Austin; numbering differs between editions). I paraphrased rather than quoted. Check the fragment number before citing it anywhere.
- **Allen's example words.** "Top-hat", "hothouse" and "blockhead" are Allen's own illustrations in *Vox Graeca*, as far as I recall. Confirm, and credit him if you keep them.
- **ει and ου.** The page gives their sounds as in Plato's time: ει a long closed *e*, ου already a long *oo*. Some teachers use [oː] for ου in the 5th century. Allen dates the change to *oo* to around the 4th century BCE.
- **Iota subscript.** "Medieval scribes" is deliberately vague. The subscript is usually dated to about the 12th century. The ι had stopped being pronounced by the later Hellenistic period.
- **ζ as *zd*.** This is the standard reconstruction for Classical Attic, but a minority of scholars argue for *dz*.
- **Pindar's heading.** One heading in the app's Pindar text prints ΣΥΡΑΚΟΥΣΙῼ, with the small iota beside the capital (a third convention). I left it out to keep the explanation simple. It could be added as an example.
- **Concise rewrite (24 Sep 2026).** The letter table was cut to one pronunciation (Classical Attic) with short cues; Erasmian and Modern Greek are summed up in one section at the end. Changed cues to check: α now "*a* in father (short or long)" (was: short = first *a* of "aha"); ι "*ee* in see" for both lengths (was: short = French *petit*); ο "*o* in hot" (was: Italian *otto*); θ φ χ now use "top / pot / kit" against "stop / spot / skit" instead of Allen's "hothouse / top-hat / blockhead"; ει "*ey* in they, held steady" (was: French *é* held long). The ἔθος/ἦθος and ἔπᾰθον examples and the Attic long-α rule (χώρᾱ) were dropped for length.
