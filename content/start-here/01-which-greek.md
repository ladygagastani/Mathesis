# Why Greek, and which Greek

Ancient Greek is the language of Homer and Sappho, of Herodotus and Thucydides, of Plato,
Aristotle and the Athenian stage, and of the New Testament. It is also the language in which
Europe first wrote history, philosophy, geometry, medicine and drama. A good translation
gives you the meaning. Only the original gives you the sound: the order of the words, and
words for which English has no single equivalent. The Greek ψυχή *psykhē* is "soul", "life"
and "ghost" all at once, and λόγος *logos* is "word", "reason" and "account". That is why
Μάθησις puts the translation *beside* the Greek, not in place of it. The translation is a
handrail; the Greek is the stair.

## A language with a long memory

Greek has been written down, with gaps, for about 3,400 years. That is the longest written
record of any language still spoken in Europe.

| Stage | When | What it is | What you can read |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mycenaean** | c. 1400–1200 BCE | The oldest written Greek, in **Linear B**, a script in which each sign stands for a syllable. It survives on clay tablets from palaces such as Knossos and Pylos, baked hard when the palaces burned. Michael Ventris showed in 1952 that the language was Greek. | Inventories and records only; no literature |
| **Homeric** | the poems took shape c. 8th–7th century BCE (the date is debated) | The language of epic: mostly Ionic, mixed with forms from other dialects and very old words kept alive by oral poetry. Nobody ever spoke it at home. | *Iliad*, *Odyssey*, Hesiod |
| **The dialects** | Archaic and Classical periods, to 323 BCE | Greece was a patchwork of city-states, each with its own **dialect** (a regional form of the language). The main literary ones are **Ionic** (the Aegean islands and the coast of Asia Minor), **Attic** (Athens), **Aeolic** (Lesbos, Boeotia, Thessaly) and **Doric** (Sparta, Corinth, Sicily and much of the Peloponnese). | Herodotus (Ionic), Sappho (Aeolic), Pindar (a literary Doric), and the whole of Athenian literature (Attic) |
| **Koine** | Hellenistic and Roman periods, c. 323 BCE – 330 CE | *Koinē* means "common". After Alexander's conquests a simplified Attic, with some Ionic features, became the shared language from Egypt to the Indus. | The Septuagint (the Greek Old Testament), the New Testament, Polybius, Plutarch, Epictetus |
| **Late Antique and Byzantine** | 330–1453 | The language of the Eastern Roman Empire. Everyday speech moved steadily towards Modern Greek, while many writers imitated Classical Attic. | The Church Fathers, Procopius, Anna Komnene |
| **Modern Greek** | after 1453 | The direct descendant of the spoken medieval language. It was made standard as *Demotic* (everyday) Greek in 1976, after long rivalry with an archaizing form. | Everything from Cavafy to today's newspapers |

## Where a beginner starts

Almost every course begins with **Attic**, the Greek of Athens in the 5th and 4th centuries
BCE. There are three good reasons:

- **Most of the classics are in it:** Plato, Thucydides, Xenophon, the orators, and the
  spoken parts of tragedy and comedy.
- **The grammars and dictionaries are built on it.** Grammar books describe Attic first, and
  dictionaries list words in their Attic form.
- **It opens the other doors.** Koine is Attic simplified, and Homer and Herodotus are best
  learned as variations on Attic. Once you know that Attic θάλαττα *thalatta* is Ionic and
  Koine θάλασσα *thalassa* ("sea"), a whole class of differences stops being mysterious.

A typical path through the texts in this app runs like this:
1. Easy Attic prose: Xenophon's *Anabasis* (where the soldiers shout θάλαττα θάλαττα,
   "The sea! The sea!", [4.7.24](read:tlg0032.tlg006:4.7.24)), and Lysias' speech *On the Murder of Eratosthenes*.
2. Plato's *Apology*.
3. Herodotus, in Ionic.
4. Homer.
5. Last, the dense choral poetry of Pindar and of tragedy.

The Gospels and the Septuagint are a gentle side door: Koine sentences are short and the
vocabulary is small.

## For review (not for publication)
- **Ventris.** "Showed in 1952 that the language was Greek": Ventris announced the decipherment in 1952, and John Chadwick joined him soon after. Decide whether Chadwick should be named here too.
- **Date range for Linear B.** "c. 1400–1200 BCE" is a round figure. The earliest Knossos tablets may belong to the later 15th century BCE, and the Pylos archive burned around 1200.
- **Doric areas.** The list (Sparta, Corinth, Sicily, much of the Peloponnese) is deliberately simplified: Crete, Rhodes and the western colonies are also Doric. Arcado-Cypriot and Northwest Greek are left out on purpose.
- **Demotic in 1976.** This is the usual date given for the language law that made Demotic standard. Confirm it if the page is ever cited formally.
- **Reading path.** Check that the works named in the path (Xenophon, Lysias 1, Plato's *Apology*, Herodotus, Homer, Pindar, the Gospels, the Septuagint) all load in the reader. They are all in the catalogue.
